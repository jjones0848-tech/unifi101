RULE #1 · BEFORE YOU TOUCH ANYTHING

Is it one device, one area, or everyone?

One device

Usually the client: weak signal, saved password, driver, or it's on the wrong network.

One area

Usually one AP, its cable, or the switch port feeding it.

Everyone

Think bigger: internet, a switch, the gateway, or a loop. Escalate fast.

Which kind of problem is it, and what do you open first in UniFi?

ANSWER

YOUR TOOLBOX

Six places in UniFi Network to look

Client Devices

Signal in dBm, band, which AP it's on, IP address, connection history.

UniFi Devices

Online or offline, firmware version, uptime, and what it's uplinked to.

Topology

How the gateway, switches and APs chain together. Spot the broken link.

Port Manager

Per switch port: link speed, PoE, port profile and VLAN, STP state.

Radios / AirView

Per AP: channel, utilization, airtime, interference and retries.

WiFiman & WiFi Agent

Test from the user's side: signal, speed and roaming where they sit.

TICKET 1

"WiFi is slow"

Look here first

  1. Client's signal (dBm). Worse than −75? It's coverage or roaming.
  2. Band and AP. On 2.4 GHz, or stuck on a far AP?
  3. That AP's AirView. High airtime or utilization?
  4. Environment tab. Neighbors on the same channel?
  5. Rule out the internet. WiFiman speed test, gateway WAN.

REAL-WORLD EXAMPLE

"Teams calls freeze in the conference room every afternoon."

Found: signal was fine, but AirView airtime spiked during the daily all-hands. One AP was carrying the whole room.

Fix: spread clients across APs and move off a crowded channel.

STEP 1 · CHECK SIGNAL FIRST

Read the dBm number

SignalWhat it meansWhat to do
−60 or stronger (−50s)ExcellentLook elsewhere.
−60 to −70AcceptableFine for normal work.
−70 to −80Weak: should roamIs a closer AP available?
−80 or weaker (−85, −90)Poor: drops likelyMove closer or add coverage.

dBm is negative: closer to zero is stronger. −55 beats −85. UniFi shows weak readings in red.

Watch out: the "Experience" score can say Excellent while the signal is bad. Trust the dBm number.

Source: Ubiquiti Help Center, WiFi Troubleshooting Guide

TICKET 2

"I can't connect to WiFi"

Look here first

  1. Search the device in Client Devices. Not listed = it never joined.
  2. Never joined? Password, SSID name, band or security type.
  3. Listed, but no IP or 169.254.x.x? WiFi worked; DHCP or VLAN didn't.
  4. Check the WiFi network's settings. Bands, WPA2 vs WPA3, PMF.

REAL-WORLD EXAMPLE

"The new smart thermostat won't join the office WiFi."

Found: never showed in Client Devices. The SSID was WPA2/WPA3 with PMF; the thermostat only does 2.4 GHz WPA2.

Fix: join it to a 2.4 GHz WPA2 IoT network.

Source: Ubiquiti Help Center, Unable to Connect to WiFi

QUICK DEFINITIONS

WPA2, WPA3 and PMF

TermWhat it isWhy it matters on a ticket
WPA2The older standard. Almost everything supports it.Use it for legacy and IoT devices.
WPA3Newer and stronger. Required on 6 GHz.Many IoT devices can't join.
PMFProtects the join and leave messages.Can't be turned off on WPA2/WPA3. Some IoT chokes on it.

The fix pattern: don't weaken the main network. Give old devices their own 2.4 GHz, WPA2 network.

Source: Ubiquiti Help Center, UniFi WiFi SSID and AP Settings Overview; Unable to Connect to WiFi

The thermostat only does 2.4 GHz and WPA2.

ANSWER

TICKET 3

"There's no WiFi"

Look here first

  1. UniFi Devices. Is the nearest AP online? If not, go to "AP is down."
  2. The WiFi network's settings. Is it broadcast on that AP and band?
  3. Hidden or scheduled? A hidden SSID or a schedule can hide it.
  4. The device. Older and IoT gear is often 2.4 GHz only.

REAL-WORLD EXAMPLE

"Warehouse scanners show no WiFi after the upgrade weekend."

Found: APs were online. The new SSID was set to 5 GHz only; the scanners only do 2.4 GHz.

Fix: turn on 2.4 GHz for that network.

TICKET 4

"The AP is down"

Look here first

  1. Its status. Offline, Adopting, or Disconnected? Since when?
  2. The LED. Use the cheat sheet on the next slide.
  3. Its switch port. Link up? PoE on? What speed?
  4. Power-cycle PoE on that port. Give it a few minutes.
  5. Stuck adopting? Check its IP, then set-inform. Reset is last.

REAL-WORLD EXAMPLE

"The break room AP has been offline since Tuesday."

Found: LED off. Its switch port showed no link. An electrician had re-terminated jacks in that hallway on Tuesday.

Fix: re-terminate the jack. AP came right back.

ROUND 8 · SCREENSHOT · 2 POINTS

Which AP has a cable problem?

Everyone answers. Write it down, then show it on 3, 2, 1.

ROUND 8 · ANSWER

Port 16: HS02 AP

It links at FE, 100 Mbps. An AP should link at gigabit. And the port shows 20 anomalies.

Why: gigabit needs all four wire pairs. Break one and the port quietly drops to 100.

Fix: test the cable and re-terminate the jack. Check HS04 AP on port 19 too.

TICKET 5

"The switch is showing offline"

Look here first

  1. Is everything behind it down too? No = management. Yes = power or uplink.
  2. Topology. Find its uplink, then check the port on the other end.
  3. Powered by PoE? Small switches often are. Check the upstream PoE.
  4. Recent change? A VLAN or IP change can strand it.

REAL-WORLD EXAMPLE

"Front office switch shows offline, but users say they're fine."

Found: the upstream port's native VLAN was changed that morning. Traffic still flowed; the switch lost its management IP.

Fix: set that port's native VLAN back. Switch reconnected.

Source: Ubiquiti Help Center, Virtual Network (VLAN) Troubleshooting

PORT MANAGER

Four things to read on any port

Link speed

10 or 100 Mbps where you expect 1 Gbps? Usually a bad cable or jack.

PoE

Is it on? Drawing watts? A maxed-out PoE budget starves devices.

Port profile / VLAN

Wrong network = "connected, no internet" or an unexpected IP range.

STP state

A "blocked" port means the switch is stopping a loop there.

NETWORK LOOPS

Whole site slow at once? Think loop.

What it looks like

  • Everyone slows down together
  • APs and switches drop in and out
  • Started right after something was plugged in

Usual suspects

  • One cable plugged into two wall jacks
  • A cheap desk switch with two uplinks
  • Sonos or mesh gear bridging wired and wireless

What to do

  • Check Ports for blocked or very busy ports
  • Undo whatever was just plugged in
  • Prevent it: BPDU Guard and Loop Protection on edge ports

Source: Ubiquiti Help Center, Understand and Mitigate Network Loops (STP)

What's happening, and where do you look?

"Monday, 9 AM. The whole office is slow and APs keep dropping out of UniFi. The only change: a new desk phone at the front desk."

ANSWER

A loop. The phone's PC port is likely cabled back into a second jack. Check the Ports page for blocked or flooded ports, then unplug the phone's second cable.
